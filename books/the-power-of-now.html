The idea in one minute

You are not the voice in your head. You are the one who hears it. Tolle's whole book follows from that sentence.

1. The mind is a useful tool that has taken over. It thinks without stopping, and we mistake its commentary for who we are.

2. That commentary lives almost entirely in the past and the future. The present moment, the only place life happens, gets used as a corridor to somewhere else.

3. Step out of the stream of thought into direct attention on what is here, and most of what you called your problems turns out to be thinking about problems.

Where it came from. Tolle describes years of anxiety and depression ending one night when he was 29. He caught himself thinking that he could not live with himself any longer, and was struck by the oddness of it: were there two of him? The question stopped his mind. He woke the next morning in a state of deep peace, and says he spent the next two years doing little but sitting on park benches. The book is written as questions and answers.

PastNowFuture

Two kinds of time. Clock time is practical: setting an appointment, learning from a mistake, planning a trip. Psychological time is living in the past or future as if they were more real than the present. He is against the second, not the first. Use the clock, then come back.
