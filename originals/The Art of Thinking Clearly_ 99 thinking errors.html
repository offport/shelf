The idea in one minute

Dobelli does not try to teach you how to think well. He lists the ways thinking reliably goes wrong, on the theory that removing errors is easier than adding brilliance. He compares it to sculpture: you get the statue by chipping away everything that is not the statue.

What makes these errors different from ordinary mistakes is that they are systematic. Everyone leans the same way, the lean is predictable, and knowing about it does not switch it off. The best you can do is recognise the situations where a given error is likely and slow down there.
